One robot, two jobs people already pay for: a pet with a personality, and a pet-and-home sitter that moves.

Why it wins

  • Real job, real budget. Pet owners already buy fixed treat cameras and pay sitters for drop-in visits. Meerlo does the check-ins every day and can go to whichever room the pet is in.
  • The periscope. One motor lifts the head 80 mm, so the camera sees over sofa cushions, toys and beds. Lowered, the brow covers the lens: a privacy shutter you can see, with no extra part.
  • Cheap by design. A single ESP32-S3 does the thinking, there is no lidar, 12 molded parts and 4 actuators. That puts the volume BOM around $44 and makes $149 retail workable.
  • Lovable on day one. One big expressive eye, ears that hear its name, a glowing belly band, and a meerkat "stand up" when something happens.

Hero features

  • ◉Patrol & check-in: scheduled rounds, then a 10-second clip of each room and pet sent to your phone.
  • ◉Treat toss: a lift-out 180 ml cup; it drops one treat at a time from its chin, from the app or as a reward.
  • ◉Sound alerts: barking, glass breaking or the smoke alarm, and it turns toward the sound and raises its head.
  • ◉Play mode: it runs away so the cat can chase it, chirps, and glows; it stops when the pet loses interest.
  • ◉Talk & see: two-way audio, night vision, and it follows you or the dog around the house.
  • ◉Self-docking: it finds the IR beacon, backs onto the spring contacts and charges itself.

Production design (DFM)

  • 12 molded parts from 11 tools: the wheel hub and its TPU tyre are one 2-shot part, mirrored left and right. Nominal wall is 2.2 mm ABS/PC, and a single horizontal split line is hidden under the light band.
  • The chassis tub has the motor saddles, battery cradle, mast tower, chute and skid molded in, so there are no brackets.
  • The mast is POM with the rack molded in. The lift motor drives it through a stock pinion, and it homes on motor stall, so there's no limit switch.
  • The treat cup is food-contact PP and lifts straight out for the dishwasher. No electronics touch food.
  • One custom 4-layer PCB, assembled on the same SMT line. The camera and eye run on flex cables down the hollow mast.
  • The radio is a pre-certified ESP32-S3 module, so FCC is part 15B only. The cell is a single 18650 (UL 2054) with protection on the board.

Assembly (8 steps, 8 screws)

  1. Drop the drive motors into the chassis saddles; push the wheels onto the D-shafts and fit the screws (2).
  2. Press in the ball caster, the battery contacts and the charging pads; snap in the cell.
  3. Fit the servo on its ribs (2 screws) and the lift motor with its pinion (2 screws).
  4. Slide the mast into the tower; plug the head flex cables into the main board.
  5. Snap the main board into its slot; connect the sensors, speaker and LED ring.
  6. Lay the light band on the tub; lower the top shell over the mast and fit 2 screws.
  7. Snap the head (face plate + head shell) onto the mast socket.
  8. Drop in the cup and rotor, close the lid, then run the end-of-line test: drive, lift, treat, camera, mics, dock.

3D-printed prototype

The same CAD prints on any 250 mm printer: PETG shells with 0.6 mm-larger snap clearances, TPU tyres, and a 0.2 mm layer for the rack. STEP and ASCII STL files are in /cad.
